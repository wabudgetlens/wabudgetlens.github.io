What the first review found

The evidence points to a more complicated story than either “Washington is wildly oversized” or “every spending increase is unavoidable.”

+36.6%

Adjusted NGF growth

The 2025–27 baseline remains materially larger than the revised 2015–17 baseline after a simple population-and-CPI adjustment, with an important fund-definition caveat.

~64%

K–12 growth partly explained

A simple run-rate comparison suggests the McCleary implementation scale explains a large share of the additional state K–12 spending that remained after the population-and-inflation adjustment.

$31.7B

Review base, not “waste”

After removing selected debt, core K–12 formula spending, and major caseload lines, the remaining review base is still heavily constrained. It is an upper bound for analysis, not a pile of discretionary spending.

Research rules

  • Use primary sources where practical.
  • Show definitions and date mismatches.
  • Separate financing shifts from real savings.
  • Distinguish workload growth from productivity.
  • Correct the record when better evidence changes the answer.
